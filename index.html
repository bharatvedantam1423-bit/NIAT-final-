What is NIAT?

NIAT (NxtWave of Innovation in Advanced Technologies) is an upskilling initiative by NxtWave that bridges the gap between industry and academia. Its industry-aligned upskilling programme is offered at 35+ universities across India.

Is the B.Tech degree awarded by NIAT?

No. You receive a UGC-recognised / AICTE-approved B.Tech from the university you enrol in, after completing its coursework, exams and credits as per UGC and AICTE guidelines. In addition, you receive an Industry Ready Certificate from NIAT. NIAT's upskilling is non-credit and does not affect degree progression.

Is the degree valid for jobs and higher studies abroad?

Yes. The B.Tech is delivered and awarded by UGC-recognised / AICTE-approved universities, so it is valid for all government and private jobs, and for higher studies in India and abroad.

What makes NIAT's upskilling programme different?
  • Deep industry integration: insights from 3,000+ companies, 10,000+ tech professionals and in-house R&D that universities use to modernise their curriculum.
  • Practitioner-led faculty support: industry-certified faculty upskilling, case-based labs and Professors of Practice who co-deliver and mentor.
  • AI-enabled experiential learning: project-based learning, live assessments and real-time feedback, modelled on top industry bootcamps.
  • Global-standard infrastructure: a research-backed classroom design playbook tailored for Indian campuses.
What is the NAT and is it mandatory?

The NxtWave Admission-counselling Test (NAT) is a 90-minute online test of problem-solving, critical thinking and mathematical ability. It is mandatory to receive admission counselling from NIAT, and it's conducted multiple times a month.

What is the NAT exam pattern?
  • Psychometric test: 44 questions · 25 min · 44 marks. No right or wrong answers.
  • Critical thinking & learnability: 20 questions · 30 min · 20 marks. Number theory, ratios, time & work, coding-decoding, blood relations, reading comprehension.
  • Mathematics: 18 questions · 35 min · 36 marks. Sets, polynomials, progressions, coordinate geometry, trigonometry, probability, P&C.

There is no negative marking.

How should I prepare for the NAT?

Solve logical reasoning problems and puzzles daily, practise JEE / CET / Olympiad-style questions, read newspapers and comprehension passages, and revise core maths concepts for understanding rather than memorisation.

What are the eligibility criteria for admission?

The NIAT team runs assessments and counselling to understand your skills, interests and aptitude, then recommends suitable universities where the programme is offered. To be admitted, you must meet the university's eligibility criteria and admission policies; admission is solely at the university's discretion.
